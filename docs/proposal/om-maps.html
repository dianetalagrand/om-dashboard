The OM App

What we are building, who for, and in what order — written from what the OM Office actually does today, taken from the Perimeter doc, the Streams Log, the Decisional Framework and the Market Architecture.

A Stream is a change to the operating model, from the moment something triggers it to the moment the operating model has absorbed it.

Section 1

What we are building

The OM Office coordinates changes to how the group is set up — which company sells what, in which market, under which tax and legal rules. It does not deliver those changes itself. It works out what a change means across Legal, Tax, DPO, Finance and the other functions, gets a decision made, then makes sure the change actually lands.

That work produces two things today: a running log of what is in flight, and a record of what was decided and why. Both are Google Docs, both are maintained by hand, and both overlap. Everything that is not written down lives in one person’s head.

The app has one job: hold that work, so that it does not depend on remembering it.

Three questions it has to answer

What people actually ask, and what answers it today
The questionWho asks itAnswered today by
Q1 “Where are we on X?” Whoever raised it, the functions, the C-Team The Streams Log — badly. Status is buried in prose.
Q2 “What was decided on Y, and why?” Anyone revisiting an old topic The Decisional Framework — well written, but you cannot search it.
Q3 “How is Z set up today, and what is in flight that would change it?” New leaders, Legal, Tax, anyone touching a market Nobody. You ask Diane.
The one that matters most

Q3 is the whole point of the Perimeter doc’s promise — new leaders get up to speed in days, not months. It is also the one that needs the most new content, so it lands last. Sections 6 and 7 are honest about that.

Section 2

Who it is for

Five groups, and only one of them writes. That is why the reading side matters as much as the working side — it serves four of the five.

Writes Diane and Nathan Run the coordination: open streams, chase the functions, escalate, keep track until the change lands. the working surface
Reads The functions Legal, Tax, DPO, Finance, HR, IT and the rest. They need to know what is being asked of them, and on what. Q1
Reads The C-Team Decide when something is escalated, and later check what was decided. They want the summary, not the working detail. Q2
Reads Whoever raised it Business or Corporate asked for something. They want to know where it got to, without asking. Q1
Reads Anyone new Needs to understand how the group is set up in a market before touching anything — and what is about to change. Q3
Already decided, worth restating

The functions never get write access. If a stream needs deeper input from someone, Diane or Nathan collect it outside the app and enter it. There is no approval step inside the app, and no per-person accounts — two people, one role.

Section 3

How OM works today

The Perimeter doc describes the flow as intake, triage, dossier, escalation. That is one direction. Across the 34 real streams the common direction is the other one: something gets decided somewhere else, and OM absorbs it.

Where it breaks today

Getting the input is chased by hand. There is no list of who owes what. It is held in memory, in Slack threads, and in whether Diane happens to remember to ask again.

Two documents, overlapping. Greece, PT and LMNext US each appear in both the Streams Log and the Decisional Framework, described differently. Keeping them consistent is manual work that produces nothing.

The current setup is not written down anywhere queryable. Which company is the Principal for Hotel NET in Spain, under which VAT rule, is knowable — by asking one person.

Section 4

What the app does

Four groups of things. Nothing here is new work invented for the app — it is the work above, held somewhere.

Take work in Open a stream and record what set it off — which table decided what, or which authority asked. Say whether it is ours, and if not, record where it went instead.
Run the coordination One line per function per stream: asked, received, blocking, or no impact. The list of who owes what stops being a memory exercise.
Get decisions made and recorded Build the escalation package out of what is already there, then record the decision, the date, the forum and the reasoning — once, in one place.
Follow through and report Track the delivery steps owned by other teams, mark when the change is in force, and produce the monthly Digest from what is already recorded.
What we are not building

Not a project tool. The Perimeter doc is explicit that OM is not a replacement for Project Managers and does not manage vertical business content. Streams carry delivery steps, but those steps belong to other teams — the app records who owns them and what is known, not who is assigned to do the work.

Also out: a screen for editing the categories, a company-wide change log, and any approval gate before a stream can close.

Section 5

The words we use

Seven words, used the same way everywhere — in the app, in this document, and when briefing Claude. Click a box to jump to its definition.

Stream A change to how the group is set up, from what triggered it to the moment it is in force. Not a project, and not just “an OM activity”. 34 of them in the Streams Log today
Decision What was chosen, why, by whom, and on what date. It either sets a stream off, or is what the stream went and got. 4 written up in the Decisional Framework
Forum The table where a decision was taken. Small thing to record, and it is how people search: “what did GDPR Core decide?” C-Team · OM Committee · OM Table · GDPR Core · ERM
Assessment One function’s input on one stream, with its state and its text. This is the thing that turns chasing into a list. 15 functions seen so far across the log
Delivery step Something that has to happen for the change to land, owned by a team outside OM. The app records the owner and what is known, not an assignment. PT system work · Greece two releases · Cruise checklists
What it changes Which parts of the setup this stream will move, picked from lists rather than typed. Recorded from the start, even before the Setup registry exists. e.g. Germany × Dynamic Package × selling company
The Setup How the group is arranged right now, per market and product: who sells, who invoices, which VAT rule, who is the data controller, which licences. Written up for France and Spain only
Digest The monthly update to stakeholders. Built from what is already recorded, then edited — rather than written from scratch each month. Listed as a main accountability in the Perimeter doc

How to read the shape

R2 is the release that changes your week. Nine things land there, and they are all things you currently hold in your head. Everything before it is preparation; everything after it is other people getting served.

Nothing is visible outside OM until R4. That is deliberate — you get to run the real work in the app for a while before anyone is looking at it.

Why R6 is last, and it is not a technical reason

The setup is only written up for France and Spain. Everywhere else, it does not exist as content yet — getting it means going round Legal, Tax and DPO market by market. No choice of tooling shortens that.

But one small thing has to be in from R2: every stream records which parts of the setup it will change, picked from lists. It costs almost nothing then, and it is the only piece that would otherwise have to be filled in again across 34 streams later. That single habit is what makes R6 a join rather than a rebuild.

Section 8

Decisions needed

Split by who can actually answer. Nothing here should be guessed by the app.

Diane — about the work itself

Is a function’s input filed under the function, or under the kind of impact? The Streams Log files it by function — “Legal:”, “Fiscal:”, “DPO:”. The C-Team package files it by impact — financial, legal, reputational.

Suggestion: file it by function, tag each one with the kind of impact, and let the C-Team view group by impact. This is the one decision that shapes everything else, so it is worth ten minutes.

Is the list of functions fixed, or can anyone add one? Fifteen show up in the log so far.

Suggestion: a fixed list you can extend deliberately. If people can type anything, the “who owes me what” screen stops being trustworthy — which is the whole value of R2.

Which parts of a market’s setup do streams actually change? Needed at R2 to build the lists, long before the setup registry exists.

From the Market Architecture: who sells, who is principal, who invoices, revenue model, VAT rule, liability model, insolvency cover, data controller, licences. Add what is missing, cut what never moves.

Does “Stream” stay the word? It behaves a bit like a project, but calling it one invites the wrong expectations — the Perimeter doc is clear OM does not replace PMs.

Suggestion: keep it. Everyone already says it, the C-Team recognises it, and 34 of them exist under that name. Change what it means, not what it is called.

Corrections to the current specs

Field by field
FieldCorrection
Driver Four values as counted above. Stays a tag on the card, not a filter.
Status Real values are TO DO, IN PROGRESS, PAUSED, DONE. The spec invented New.
DetailSections Becomes one row per function per stream, each with an owner, a state and its text — not five fixed categories plus an ancillary. Fifteen functions observed.
Completeness % Stops being a manual slider. Derived twice: how much input is in, and how much delivery is done.
Dates Three, all distinct: when the decision was taken, when the change came into force (repeatable — phased landings), and any technical go-live. Closing the stream is a fourth, separate event. Mapping Effective date onto EndDate was wrong.
Trigger New, and the biggest gap: origin type plus a link to the decision and forum that set the stream off. Absent from the current spec.
Requester Absorbed into the trigger. It was already only descriptive.
Link to the setup New: market × product × attribute tags, from R2, against controlled lists. The registry itself waits for R6.
What survives from the existing specs, unchanged

Every decision about how the app behaves carries over: one Admin role for both of you, no login with read access on the network, one-way publish, the drag board with manual ordering, read-only history, no delete and no rollback, two audiences for notifications, categories stay in a config file.

What changes is the shape of the data and the category list underneath — not the thinking about behaviour.
